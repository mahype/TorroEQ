# Sony's Segmented Displays Make Spectrum Legible

Sony equalizers and spectrum analyzers around 1988-1994 looked like compact instruments: **narrow stacks of discrete luminous blocks floated in a smoked-black recess, with hard dark gaps between frequency bands, tiny fixed labels, and restrained cyan, blue-white, amber, or red accents**. The closest direct model for TorroEQ is the 1988 SEQ-333ES, whose ten analyzer columns correspond one-for-one to its ten EQ bands; the 1992-1994 SEQ-711 best captures the busier "many small lights" effect through two independently moving seven-band channel displays. TorroEQ should therefore replace its continuous spectrum with exactly ten non-interpolated segmented columns, one for each adjustable band, and preserve empty space horizontally and vertically so no neighboring values blur into a single silhouette. Each column should have independent dB-domain motion, fast attack, slower release, and one detached peak-hold marker. A near-black field, one cool body color, and a tiny amber/red upper warning zone will reproduce the period hierarchy more convincingly than glow, gradients, or a frequency rainbow. For a terminal implementation, a custom cell renderer using `▄` segments, compact labels, stable scaling, and an ASCII fallback provides both the Sony character and reliable readability.

## Sony made black space part of the display

The period design was not a smooth spectral plot rendered with fewer pixels. **Sony discretized both dimensions:** horizontal position meant a fixed frequency band, while vertical position meant a stepped level. The SEQ-333ES used ten approximately octave-spaced centers - 31.5, 63, 125, 250, and 500 Hz, then 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 kHz - and its product image shows ten narrow segmented stacks in a long black recess ([Audio Database](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html)). The SEQ-711 placed two seven-column plots under explicit `LEFT` and `RIGHT` headings, with every column divided into short horizontal luminous blocks and every adjacent band separated by a dark vertical gutter ([Fein-HiFi](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html)). Demonstration footage confirms that neighboring stacks changed to different heights without a connecting line or luminous bridge, so the display read as independent measurements rather than a filled waveform ([SEQ-711 demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQaMxxjVp2U)).

The surrounding fascia reinforced that legibility. Sony put low-brightness graphics behind glossy smoked windows, printed small frequency legends directly below stable column positions, and kept controls outside the moving plot. The SEQ-711 favored pale cool phosphor; the SEQ-D705 separated cyan data graphics from amber mode text and red indicators; the SEQ-V901 used warm amber/yellow stacks with red scale accents and an explicitly advertised peak-hold function ([Darthy SEQ-D705 archive](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/); [SEQ-V901 demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)). Photographs cannot establish exact factory colors, and video cannot recover calibrated animation timing, but the consistent visual grammar is clear: **black field, repeated segment geometry, categorical bands, sparse text, and accent color reserved for status or exceptional level**.

## Ten invariant columns should replace interpolation

TorroEQ should map its controls directly to the SEQ-333ES pattern, labeled `31`, `62` or `63`, `125`, `250`, `500`, `1k`, `2k`, `4k`, `8k`, and `16k`. The displayed value for each position must come only from that band's analysis state; **do not interpolate a connecting curve, fill space between bands, merge bands at narrow widths, or normalize each band independently**. Period documentation for the AudioSource EQ Eleven describes the same functional model - ten LED columns corresponding directly to ten EQ controls and showing relative energy in those bands - which confirms that one-column-per-control was a practical 1990s analyzer convention, not merely decoration ([EQ Eleven manual, p. 4](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/450054/Audiosource-Eq-11.html?page=4#manual)).

Render 12-16 whole segment rows over a fixed logarithmic range, with at least one completely dark cell between columns. At a 72-column terminal width, a robust layout is ten five-cell bands plus nine one-cell gutters, a four-cell dB scale, and centered side margins; above 92 columns, increase gutters to two cells and cap band width at seven cells so the display does not become ten heavy slabs. Labels should remain compact and fixed through resizing. A useful 12-row default maps each row to 3 dB for a 36 dB window and labels only `0`, `-12`, `-24`, and `-36 dB`; 3 dB steps are consistent with conventional LM3915 logarithmic LED meters, though Sony's public material does not document its analyzer range ([Elliott Sound Products](https://www.sound-au.com/project60.htm)). The EQ units' advertised +/-10 or +/-12 dB figures describe adjustment range and must not be mistaken for analyzer calibration.

Use a custom Ratatui widget rather than forcing this geometry through a continuous bar chart. Draw each active row with repeated `▄` cells: its lower-half fill leaves a dark horizontal slit above each segment, creating VFD-like separation without consuming a blank terminal row. Render off segments as spaces for maximum figure-ground contrast, or as very dim instances of the same glyph only when a persistent physical grid suits the theme. Use a centered `-` run for the peak, and provide an explicit ASCII mode with `=` for live segments and `-` for peaks. Ratatui's buffer supports symbol and style changes per cell plus minimal frame diffs, while Unicode width rules cannot guarantee identical glyph artwork across every terminal and font; validating one-cell width and retaining ASCII fallback are therefore essential ([Ratatui Buffer](https://docs.rs/ratatui/latest/ratatui/buffer/struct.Buffer.html); [Unicode TR11](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr11/)).

## Restrained color turns movement into information

The primary preset should use **near-black background, cyan or blue-white live segments, dim blue-gray labels and scales, amber only for the upper two rows, and red only at the top reference or for an over-reference peak**. This follows the hierarchy of the cool SEQ-711 and SEQ-D705 rather than copying uncertain photographed hues. A second historically grounded preset can use an amber body, yellow high zone, and red peaks or outer scale after the SEQ-V901. Named ANSI colors should be the compatibility baseline; RGB color should remain optional because Ratatui notes that true-color output depends on terminal support ([Ratatui Color](https://docs.rs/ratatui/latest/ratatui/style/enum.Color.html)).

Color must describe level, never frequency. A bass-to-treble rainbow would turn ten comparable measurements into decorative categories and weaken the one-to-one relationship with the EQ controls. Likewise, simulated glow in neighboring cells, fractional block ramps, bright grid lines, mirrored reflections, numeric values over every bar, and scrolling history all consume the negative space that made the hardware readable. Use the gutters as the horizontal grid, keep the frame and baseline faint, and place mode or status text outside the moving field. If stereo comparison is essential, show two complete stacked ten-band panes labeled `L` and `R`; do not squeeze two values into each band. Otherwise combine channels before analysis with a consistent power-based or higher-channel rule and retain one unambiguous indication per adjustable band.

## Independent ballistics create crisp musical motion

Each band needs its own continuous state before quantization. Smooth in dB space with `shown += (1 - exp(-dt / tau)) * (target - shown)`, using **45 ms attack and 220 ms release** as the balanced starting point, then quantize to segment rows with roughly 0.2 segment of hysteresis. This preserves transient rise while preventing boundary chatter; smoothing after quantization would instead create delayed steps. Render at 30 frames per second by default, derive all motion from elapsed time, and let Ratatui emit only changed cells. Web Audio defines the same exponential previous/current averaging model and notes that no smoothing produces noticeably jarring motion, while CAVA independently demonstrates buffered terminal updates and separates visual frame rate from analysis behavior ([Web Audio specification](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/#fft-windowing-and-smoothing-over-time); [CAVA reference configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karlstav/cava/master/example_files/config)). If TorroEQ's analyzer already smooths heavily upstream, reduce the display smoother rather than stacking two sluggish filters.

Maintain a separate peak position and timer for every band. When the live value reaches a new maximum, move the marker immediately and hold it for **550 ms**; afterward let it descend at about **14 dB/s** until it rejoins the live stack. Keep the marker narrower than the body and suppress it while it touches the live top so it communicates "recent maximum" rather than becoming another cap. Period metering supports the principle of a rapidly responding level plus a slower, detached peak indication, and the SEQ-V901 explicitly documents peak hold, but the proposed constants are TorroEQ engineering defaults rather than recovered Sony specifications ([VU and PPM Audio Metering](https://www.sound-au.com/project55.htm); [SEQ-V901 references](https://search.brave.com/search?q=%22Sony+SEQ-V901%22&source=web)). At silence, let bands release naturally and peaks complete their decay; do not add idle animation. The desired coolness comes from coherent response to sound, not perpetual visual noise.

## Conclusion

The decisive shift is conceptual: TorroEQ should stop treating the spectrum as a low-resolution picture and start treating it as **ten small instruments sharing one panel**. Stable band identity, darkness between readings, and independently timed peak memory will reveal musical balance more clearly than additional resolution or blur effects can.

This design is also testable. Pink noise should produce a broadly level ten-column field, an octave-band tone should dominate one column with plausible neighboring leakage, a click should create a quick rise and detached held peak, and silence should clear without flicker. Passing those checks matters more than exact imitation of undocumented Sony phosphor color or timing; it preserves the functional discipline that made the original displays memorable.

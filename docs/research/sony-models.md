# Sony Consumer Equalizer and Spectrum-Display Design, c. 1988-1999

## Which Sony models best match the memory of many small lights moving independently?

### Takeaway
The strongest match is the Sony SEQ-711 (1992-94): its fluorescent display can show separate left/right seven-band spectrum plots, producing 14 independently changing segmented columns. The 1988 SEQ-333ES is the best direct precedent for a 10-band redesign; the SEQ-V901 is the best warm-color/peak-hold alternative, while the SEQ-D705 is more curve-editor and surround-processor than "many small lights." - [SEQ-711 documentation](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-333ES model page](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html); [SEQ-V901 demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q); [SEQ-D705 photographs](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/)

### Cited Findings
- **SEQ-711 (1992-94), strongest memory match.** Sony's operating-instruction index identifies """Changing the Display""" and """Demo Mode,""" while a specialist listing describes a switchable display for channel-separated spectrum analysis or curve display. The front photograph shows two clearly separated plots labeled `LEFT` and `RIGHT`, each with seven narrow segmented columns. A demonstration shows the two sets of stacks changing independently with music. This is a Sony product, not a look-alike from another manufacturer. - [Sony operating instructions](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/687250/Sony-Seq-711.html); [Fein-HiFi description and photographs](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [video demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQaMxxjVp2U)
- **SEQ-711 display construction (image observation).** A deep smoked-black window contains two compact rectangular plotting areas with a wide central gutter. Each plot has seven equal-width columns made from discrete horizontal blocks, small frequency legends below, and a channel heading above. Pale blue-white/lilac phosphor is dominant in the clearest listing photograph; some blocks appear pinkish, but photographs and camera white balance do not establish the exact factory phosphor color. - [Fein-HiFi front/detail photographs](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html)
- **SEQ-333ES (released 1988), best 10-band reference.** It is a Sony 10-band graphic equalizer with a real-time analyzer. Its centers are `31.5, 63, 125, 250, 500 Hz, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 kHz`, with a +/-12 dB adjustment range. The product photograph shows ten narrow, visibly segmented vertical stacks in a long black display recess, with tiny labels beneath and hard separation between columns. - [Audio Database model page and photograph](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html)
- **SEQ-V901, strongest warm-color alternative.** Listings identify it as a Sony seven-band equalizer with a """double spectrum analyzer""" and peak hold. The video shows two analyzer areas above two banks of seven physical sliders: warm amber/yellow stacked blocks, red vertical scale marks at the outside edge of each plot, and bright upper blocks that remain visually distinct from the body of a column. Its exact production year was not established from a primary source in this pass. - [model search result with service-manual and listing references](https://search.brave.com/search?q=%22Sony+SEQ-V901%22&source=web); [video demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)
- **SEQ-D705 (1991), weaker memory match but useful color reference.** This is a Sony seven-band digital graphic equalizer with centers at `63, 160, 400 Hz, 1, 2.5, 6.3, 15 kHz` and +/-10 dB control. Clear photographs and a demonstration show a wide smoked display split into left and right seven-band areas, primarily cyan/turquoise graphics, with amber/orange mode text in the center and small red status indicators on the faceplate. Its broad cyan columns and editable curves read as larger graphic elements rather than a field of tiny lamps. - [AudioBaza specifications, photograph, and manual links](https://audiobaza.blogspot.com/2015/04/sony-seq-d705.html); [Darthy archive photographs and specifications](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/); [video demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yq2kIhlNuA4)
- **SEQ-555ES is lineage, not an in-range answer.** It dates to about 1984, before the requested period, although its analyzer and automatic room-correction functions help explain the later SEQ-333ES design. It should not be presented as a 1988-99 model. - [Audio Database SEQ-555ES page](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-555es.html)

### Inferences
- If the remembered display looked like two busy banks of tiny pale lights, the SEQ-711 is the most plausible identification because stereo separation creates 14 independently moving stacks rather than one seven-column silhouette.
- If the memory instead includes physical sliders and red/amber illumination, the SEQ-V901 is the better match; if it includes no sliders and ten tightly spaced bands, the SEQ-333ES is more likely.
- Camera capture of VFDs is unreliable for hue and brightness. Treat the observed palettes as families (`cool pale phosphor` versus `warm amber/red`), not sampled RGB specifications.

### Gaps
- No Sony catalog or primary manual located in this pass established the SEQ-V901 introduction year or the exact duration/decay law of its peak hold.
- The sources found did not establish measured analyzer refresh rates, attack times, decay times, exact segment counts, or calibrated phosphor colors for any model.
- Reliable evidence was sparse for memorable standalone Sony equalizers introduced in the later 1995-99 part of the window; the clearest representatives cluster in 1988-94. This does not prove Sony sold none later.

## How were spectrum bands visually divided and labeled?

### Takeaway
Sony repeatedly used logarithmically spaced audio bands, a blank gutter between every column, tiny legends directly below the plotting baseline, and strong separation between left and right channels. The SEQ-333ES supplies a ready-made 10-band frequency set; the SEQ-711 and SEQ-D705 show how to frame and label stereo plots without turning them into one continuous shape. - [SEQ-333ES](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html); [SEQ-711](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-D705](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/)

### Cited Findings
- **Ten-band spacing on the SEQ-333ES:** `31.5 | 63 | 125 | 250 | 500 | 1k | 2k | 4k | 8k | 16k`. This is approximately octave spacing and spans low bass through the top audible octave. The analyzer columns align one-for-one with these equalizer centers. - [Audio Database](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html)
- **Seven-band spacing on the SEQ-711:** `63 | 160 | 400 | 1k | 2.5k | 6.3k | 16k`, with +/-10 dB equalization. Photographs place one abbreviated frequency label beneath each column and repeat the full set under both `LEFT` and `RIGHT` plots. - [Fein-HiFi](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [HiFi-Wiki](https://hifi-wiki.com/index.php/Sony_SEQ-711)
- **Seven-band spacing on the SEQ-D705:** `63 | 160 | 400 | 1k | 2.5k | 6.3k | 15k`. The photographed display prints `(Hz)` before each channel's sequence and `RANGE` after it, while `LEFT` and `RIGHT` headings sit high above the data. - [AudioBaza](https://audiobaza.blogspot.com/2015/04/sony-seq-d705.html); [Darthy front photograph](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/)
- **Segment and gap treatment (image observation).** The SEQ-711 renders each value as a stack of short horizontal luminous blocks, not as a solid rectangle. A dark horizontal slit separates every level, a larger dark vertical gutter separates adjacent frequency columns, and an especially wide blank area separates channels. The SEQ-333ES uses the same small-block principle in a denser ten-column field. - [SEQ-711 detail photograph](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-333ES photograph](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html)
- **Scales and framing (image observation).** The SEQ-V901 places a red dotted vertical level scale at the outer side of each analyzer and uses warm block stacks inside; the SEQ-711 instead relies on faint rectangular plot boundaries, a baseline, labels, and channel headings. The SEQ-D705 adds dotted vertical range scales near the outer sides of its left/right plotting areas. - [SEQ-V901 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q); [SEQ-711 photographs](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-D705 photographs](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/)
- **Bezel and background treatment (image observation).** All four primary examples put low-brightness graphics behind a glossy smoked window in an otherwise black or charcoal faceplate. Labels are small and restrained; controls sit outside the plot or in a separate control block, so the luminous area reads as an instrument panel rather than decoration. - [SEQ-333ES](https://audio-database.com/SONY-ESPRIT/etc/seq-333es.html); [SEQ-711](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-D705](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/); [SEQ-V901 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)

### Inferences
- For a 10-band terminal EQ, use the SEQ-333ES centers but abbreviate them to a fixed-width row: `31  63  125 250 500  1k  2k  4k  8k  16k`. Preserve at least one completely dark terminal cell between columns.
- A single 10-column analyzer is the most faithful compact translation of the SEQ-333ES. If width permits, two 10-column plots labeled `L` and `R`, separated by a 3-5 cell gutter, better reproduce the """many independent lights""" effect of the SEQ-711.
- Use a 12-16-row plot with two-character-wide illuminated blocks. These dimensions are terminal design recommendations, not verified Sony hardware dimensions.

### Gaps
- Source photographs are not sufficiently orthogonal and high-resolution to count every VFD row confidently. The visible evidence supports """roughly ten or more discrete levels,""" not a defensible exact segment count.
- The SEQ-711 manual page images were available through a third-party manual host, but its OCR did not expose the full body text for the display-mode instructions; switchable analyzer/curve behavior is corroborated by the manual index and specialist description rather than quoted verbatim.

## Which visual and motion details kept the displays legible rather than a continuous filled shape?

### Takeaway
Legibility came from negative space and hierarchy: discrete blocks, dark inter-column gutters, independent stereo frames, sparse labels, subdued baselines/scales, and occasional isolated peak cues. A terminal version should preserve those separations and use color as a restrained status layer, not paint every cell into a rainbow area graph. - [SEQ-711 photographs](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html); [SEQ-V901 demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)

### Cited Findings
- **Independent columns, not interpolation.** In the SEQ-711 demonstration, neighboring stacks repeatedly occupy different heights in both channels; no luminous bridge connects their tops. The changing pattern therefore reads as individual frequency measurements rather than a filled waveform. - [SEQ-711 video demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQaMxxjVp2U)
- **Channel duplication creates order.** The SEQ-711 repeats the same frequency labels and plot geometry under explicit `LEFT` and `RIGHT` headings. The mirrored structure makes 14 moving columns easier to parse than one 14-column undifferentiated field. - [Fein-HiFi detail photograph](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html)
- **Peak hold is explicitly associated with the SEQ-V901.** Auction/listing descriptions call it a """7-Band Peak Hold Double Spectrum Analyser,""" and demonstration footage shows bright high blocks visually separated from darker lower stacks in changing music passages. The listing wording verifies the function; the footage verifies the warm red/amber presentation, but neither source supplies hold time. - [search results and listing references](https://search.brave.com/search?q=%22Sony+SEQ-V901%22&source=web); [SEQ-V901 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)
- **Display modes prevent overload.** The SEQ-711 operating-instruction contents separately list """Checking the Equalization Results,""" """Changing the Display,""" and """Demo Mode"""; specialist documentation states that its fluorescent display switches between channel-separated spectrum analysis and curve display. It does not superimpose every function continuously. - [Sony operating instructions](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/687250/Sony-Seq-711.html); [Fein-HiFi](https://www.fein-hifi.de/Equalizer/Sony/Sony-SEQ-711-Highend-7-Band-Grafik-Equalizer::3013.html)
- **Restricted color roles.** The SEQ-D705 photographs distinguish cyan data graphics from amber/orange mode text and small red status lamps; the SEQ-V901 uses amber/yellow data against red scales. In both, the background remains nearly black and most unlit display area stays empty. - [SEQ-D705 photographs](https://www.darthy.com/en/product/sony-seq-d705/); [SEQ-V901 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUL_T4VLP5Q)

### Inferences
- **Recommended 10-band terminal layout:** one dark cell gutter between two-cell columns; 12-16 discrete vertical levels; dim axis/label color; ten fixed labels based on the SEQ-333ES; no connecting line and no horizontal fill between bands.
- **Recommended palette:** near-black background; cool cyan or blue-white main blocks after the SEQ-711/SEQ-D705; dim blue-gray labels and frame; optional amber warning zone only in the top 2-3 rows. A second authentic preset could use SEQ-V901-style amber bodies, yellow high blocks, and red scales/peaks.
- **Recommended peak treatment:** draw one isolated bright cell above each column, hold it for about 0.5-1.0 seconds, then let it descend more slowly than the body. Peak hold is historically evidenced on the SEQ-V901, but these timing values are implementation choices because no Sony timing specification was found.
- **Recommended motion:** fast attack, stepped fall over several frames, and independent smoothing per band and channel. This reproduces the visibly independent movement in demonstrations without claiming Sony's undocumented filter constants.
- **Recommended bezel treatment:** put the plot inside a charcoal box with a one-cell inner margin, a faint baseline, and either a very dim rectangular frame (SEQ-711) or dotted outer level scale (SEQ-V901/D705). Keep mode/status text outside the moving grid.
- **Avoid:** solid area fills, touching columns, gradients across every band, equally bright labels and data, constantly visible EQ curves over the analyzer, or rapid full-column flicker. Those choices remove the negative space and hierarchy visible in the cited Sony examples.

### Gaps
- No source found quantified Sony's analyzer ballistics, peak-hold timeout, peak decay rate, or whether attack/decay differed by model.
- Video compression, exposure, and VFD multiplex interaction can create false gaps, streaking, or color shifts. Motion conclusions here are limited to clearly repeated column-height changes and explicitly documented peak-hold/display modes.
